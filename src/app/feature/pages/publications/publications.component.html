Inference-Based Localization of Primary Users in Geolocation Database-Assisted Spectrum Sharing
(Under Review)

Abstract

The rapid proliferation of wireless devices and bandwidth-intensive applications has exacerbated spectrum scarcity, accelerating the adoption of dynamic spectrum access technologies. Cognitive radio networks (CRNs) relying on geolocation databases (GDBs) allow secondary users (SUs) to opportunistically utilize licensed bands while safeguarding primary users (PUs) from harmful interference. However, these database-centric architectures introduce severe privacy vulnerabilities. This paper investigates these privacy risks by demonstrating that an adversarial SU can covertly infer and track a mobile PU’s precise location by systematically exploiting the Maximum Transmit Power (MTP) responses returned by the GDB. To rigorously assess this threat, we evaluate two complementary location inference frameworks: a Bayesian recursive tracking approach based on Particle Filtering (PF) for dynamic, real-time estimation, and a data-driven supervised Machine Learning (ML) approach for offline localization. Extensive simulation results confirm the high efficacy of both methods under realistic operational conditions. In the PF framework, utilizing misclassification error as an impurity-based convergence metric ensures optimal localization accuracy, outperforming entropy and Gini impurities by 73.85% and 6.29%, respectively. Within the ML framework, Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) exhibit superior generalization and the lowest error rates among evaluated classifiers; comparatively, Support Vector Machines (SVMs) yielded up to 26.44% higher Mean Squared Error (MSE) and 39.93% higher cross-validation loss. These findings expose a critical, inherently structural privacy flaw in current spectrum-sharing systems, underscoring that standard interference-prevention mechanisms can be maliciously leveraged for highly accurate spatial profiling.


Keywords: Cognitive Radio Networks, Primary User Privacy, Secondary User, Geolocation Database, Particle Filter, Machine Learning, Location Inference Attack

Differential Privacy Mechanisms for Geo-Location Database Responses in Cognitive Radio Networks
(Under Review)

Abstract

Geo-location databases (GDBs) play a critical role in dynamic spectrum access (DSA) by providing maximum transmission power (MTP) values to secondary users (SUs) based on their proximity to primary users (PUs). However, recent studies have demonstrated that sequential MTP responses can be exploited to infer PU locations through particle filtering and machine learning techniques, posing severe privacy risks. In this paper, we propose a differential privacy (DP) framework for GDB responses that preserves PU location privacy while maintaining spectrum utilization efficiency. Our approach introduces calibrated Laplace noise to MTP values based on the sensitivity of the distance-power mapping function. We present a comprehensive privacy-utility trade-off analysis, demonstrating that moderate privacy budgets (ε = 0.5–1.0) can effectively thwart location inference attacks while incurring minimal degradation in spectrum efficiency. Through extensive simulations, we show that our DP-MTP protocol increases the root mean square error (RMSE) of particle filter-based attacks from near-zero to over 50 meters, rendering inference impractical. Furthermore, we introduce an adaptive noise injection mechanism that adjusts privacy protection based on SU query density, achieving superior performance in heterogeneous network scenarios. Our results confirm that differential privacy provides a mathematically rigorous and practical solution for privacy-preserving GDB design in cognitive radio networks.


Keywords: Differential Privacy, Geo-location Database, Cognitive Radio, Spectrum Sharing, Location Privacy, Maximum Transmission Power
